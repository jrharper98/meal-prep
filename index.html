The rotation

Day 1 is Monday. Days 1–3 live in the fridge; days 4–7 live in the freezer until the night before. Label every lid with week, day and meal, like "Wk A D4 lunch", so day numbers don't get mixed up with recipe codes (A1, B2…).

Week A

Week B

About the numbers. Totals include your shake (1 scoop Ascent chocolate + 1 cup 2% milk: 242 kcal, 33 g protein), ¼ cup kimchi, one cup of broth, one cup of ginger tea with honey, and a teaspoon of honey in your focus tea. Days 1–3 use the gym breakfast (Siggi's vanilla, ½ cup trail mix, 1 tbsp chia). Days 4–7 use the prepped breakfast plus ¼ cup trail mix in the afternoon. They're estimates from USDA values, so expect about ±10% depending on brands and portions.

Protein lands around 150–165 g, above your 120 g floor. If you'd rather sit closer to 120, the easiest swap is less meat in each batch and more beans or lentils.

Fiber runs about 27–42 g; the shrimp stir-fry days in Week B are the low end. If that's a jump from what you eat now, drink extra water the first week or two; bloating usually settles as your gut adjusts.

Sunday prep

Two cooking sessions alternate: Week A prep follows the big shopping trip, Week B prep follows the quick stop. Your broth base and ginger tea can simmer on a back burner during either one.

The rule for every container

  • Days 1–3: fridge. Days 4–7: freezer.
  • Sauce cups (tahini, dill yogurt, labneh) go wherever their meal goes.
  • Cool food fast before lidding: spread it on sheet pans for 20 minutes, then into the fridge.
  • Glass in the freezer: straight-sided jars with about 1½ inches of headspace.

Week A prep, about 2 hours 20 minutes

Makes 14 mains (5 × A1, 5 × A2, 4 × A3), 4 oats jars (Days 4–7), 12 egg muffins, a batch of pesto, and the vinaigrette.

  1. 0:00 Mince all the garlic (11 cloves) and let it sit 10 minutes. Oven to 425°F. Start ⅓ cup quinoa.
  2. 0:05 Cube sweet potatoes, toss with oil and spices, into the oven for a 10-minute head start. Season the chicken thighs; trim the shiitake and broccoli.
  3. 0:15 Chicken and shiitake go in on a second pan. Add broccoli to the sweet potato pan. Start the chili on the stove.
  4. 0:40 A1 pans out. Cauliflower goes in. Lentils and beans should already be simmering in the chili.
  5. 0:45 Chop the salmon and mix the cakes with the cooled quinoa. Let the flax bind for 5 minutes while you blend the pesto and whisk the tahini sauce, dill yogurt and vinaigrette.
  6. 0:55 Salmon cakes into the oven. Sprinkle black seed on the cauliflower. Chili comes off the heat when the lentils are tender.
  7. 1:15 Cakes and cauliflower out. Drizzle vinaigrette on the cauliflower. Drop the oven to 350°F with the door cracked for a minute.
  8. 1:20 Sauté the egg muffin vegetables, whisk the eggs, fill the tin, bake 20–22 minutes. Assemble the 4 oats jars while they bake.
  9. 1:45 Everything cools on sheet pans. Spoon pesto into an ice cube tray (1 tablespoon per cube) and freeze.
  10. 1:55 Portion and label. Fridge: 6 mains, 6 muffins, sauce cups for days 1–3. Freezer: 8 mains, 4 oats jars, 6 muffins, sauce cups for days 4–7, pesto tray.
  11. 2:20 Restock the work stash bins (list under Tea, broth & stash).

Week B prep, about 1 hour 50 minutes

Makes 14 mains (5 × B1, 5 × B2, 4 × B3), 8 baked oat cups (Days 4–7), and 12 egg muffins. Chicken moves from freezer to fridge on Friday night; thaw the shrimp in cold water while you start.

  1. 0:00 Mince all the garlic (8 cloves) and let it sit. Oven to 425°F. Big pot of water on to boil for the rice.
  2. 0:05 Marinate the chicken breast. Season the thighs. Two B2 pans into the oven: chicken with onion, and carrots with chickpeas.
  3. 0:10 Rice into the boiling water, pasta-style, for 30 minutes.
  4. 0:15 Cook B1 in a big skillet: mushrooms, then chicken, then cabbage.
  5. 0:35 B2 pans out; drizzle pomegranate molasses. Drain the rice, lid it for 10 minutes. Oven to 350°F.
  6. 0:40 Mix the oat cups and bake 22–25 minutes. Put the soba water on.
  7. 0:45 Cook the soba one minute short and rinse it cold. Cook B3 in the wok.
  8. 1:05 Oat cups out, egg muffins in (20–22 minutes). Finish B1 with the miso-sesame mix off the heat. Stir the garlic into the labneh.
  9. 1:15 Spread the rice on a tray to cool quickly. Cooked rice should be in the fridge within about an hour.
  10. 1:30 Portion and label. Fridge: 6 mains, 6 muffins, sauce cups for days 1–3. Freezer: 8 mains, 8 oat cups, 6 muffins, sauce cups for days 4–7. Pull 5 pesto cubes into a bag labeled B2.

Recipes

Each main cooks in 45 minutes or less. Nutrition is per serving, including its sauce.

Garlic, every time: mince it first and let it sit 10 minutes before heat or acid touches it. Sauces with raw garlic (tahini, labneh, pesto) stay cold and go on after reheating, never before.

Week A

A1: Sheet-pan chicken, shiitake, broccoli & sweet potato

5 servings. about 485 kcal, 37 g protein, 11 g fiber. 40 minutes

  • 1½ lb boneless skinless chicken thighs
  • 1½ lb sweet potatoes, ¾-inch cubes
  • Fall and winter: swap in butternut or kabocha squash for half the sweet potato.
  • 1½ lb broccoli florets
  • 12 oz shiitake, stems removed, halved
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 1 tsp each smoked paprika, cumin and garlic powder; ½ tsp turmeric; black pepper; salt
  • Sauce: 5 tbsp tahini, ¼ cup lemon juice, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp honey, salt, 3–5 tbsp water
  1. Oven to 425°F. Mince the sauce garlic and let it rest.
  2. Toss sweet potatoes with ½ tbsp oil and half the spices. Roast 10 minutes.
  3. Toss chicken with the rest of the spices and ½ tbsp oil; spread on a second pan with the shiitake. Add broccoli with the last ½ tbsp oil to the sweet potato pan.
  4. Roast both pans 20–25 minutes, until the chicken reads 175°F (safe at 165°F; thighs are juicier higher).
  5. Whisk the sauce until pourable. Portion into 5 small cups.
  6. Slice the chicken and portion. Top with ground papaya seed after reheating.
A2: Turkey, lentil & cremini chili

5 servings. about 556 kcal, 41 g protein, 16 g fiber (with 1 pesto cube). 40 minutes. freezes best

  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 lb ground turkey (93% lean)
  • 1 onion and 2 carrots, diced
  • 1 lb 4 oz cremini, chopped small
  • Summer and fall: swap a diced bell pepper in for one carrot.
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 6 oz can tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp chili powder, 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp Mexican oregano
  • ¾ cup dry green or brown lentils, rinsed
  • 2 cans black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 cups rosemary-sage broth base (or water)
  • 1 tsp dried epazote (in summer, a few fresh sprigs instead)
  • Salt, lime juice
  • To serve: 1 frozen pesto cube per bowl
  1. Mince the garlic first. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven and brown the turkey, about 5 minutes.
  2. Add onion, carrots and mushrooms. Cook 6–8 minutes, until the mushroom liquid cooks off.
  3. Add garlic, tomato paste and spices. Stir 2 minutes, until the paste darkens.
  4. Add lentils, beans, broth and epazote. Cover and simmer about 25 minutes, until the lentils are tender.
  5. Pull the epazote stems, then season with salt and lime.
  6. Pack a frozen pesto cube in a small cup next to each container. Stir it in after heating.
A3: Salmon-quinoa cakes with roasted cauliflower

4 servings (12 cakes). about 515 kcal, 38 g protein, 8 g fiber. 40 minutes

  • 1 lb skinless wild salmon
  • 1 cup cooked quinoa (⅓ cup dry), cooled
  • 2 eggs, ¼ cup Greek yogurt, 2 tbsp ground flax
  • ½ small onion, grated
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp chopped dill, zest of 1 lemon, 1 tsp salt, black pepper
  • If dill is out: flat-leaf parsley, or 1 tsp dried dill in the cakes.
  • 1 tbsp olive oil for brushing
  • Cauliflower: 2 heads in florets, 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp cumin, salt, 1 tsp black seed
  • Late summer, when cauliflower is thin: broccoli, same weight. Fall and winter: half cauliflower, half winter squash.
  • Finish: 2 tsp pomegranate vinaigrette per serving
  • Dill yogurt: ½ cup Greek yogurt, 1 tbsp lemon juice, 1 tbsp dill, salt
  1. Oven to 425°F. Roast the cauliflower with oil, cumin and salt for 25–30 minutes; add the black seed for the last 5.
  2. Chop the salmon into ¼-inch pieces by knife (or a few quick pulses in a processor).
  3. Mix salmon with quinoa, eggs, yogurt, flax, onion, garlic, dill, zest, salt and pepper. Rest 5 minutes.
  4. Shape 12 patties on a parchment-lined pan and brush with oil. Bake 15–18 minutes, to 145°F inside.
  5. Drizzle the vinaigrette on the cauliflower once it's out of the oven.
  6. Stir together the dill yogurt and portion into 4 cups. Eat the fridge servings by day 3.

Week B

B1: Miso-ginger chicken, oyster mushrooms & cabbage over brown rice

5 servings. about 490 kcal, 41 g protein, 8 g fiber. 45 minutes

  • 1¼ cups brown rice
  • 1½ lb chicken breast, thinly sliced
  • Marinade: 1 tbsp white miso, 2 tbsp tamari, 1 tbsp honey, 2 tbsp grated ginger (or 1 tsp Nigerian ginger powder), 3 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp avocado oil
  • 12 oz oyster mushrooms, torn
  • 1½ lb cabbage, shredded
  • Finish: 2 tbsp white miso, 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tbsp hot water, 1 tbsp sesame seeds, scallions
  1. Boil the rice in a big pot of water like pasta for 30 minutes. Drain, then cover for 10 minutes.
  2. Mince the garlic, let it rest, then marinate the chicken for at least 10 minutes.
  3. Heat oil in a large skillet over high heat. Brown the mushrooms, 5–6 minutes, then set aside.
  4. Cook the chicken 6–7 minutes, to 165°F.
  5. Add cabbage and a splash of water; cook 4–5 minutes until just wilted. Return the mushrooms.
  6. Off the heat, stir in the miso-sesame finish and the sesame seeds.
  7. Cool the rice fast on a tray before portioning.

Miso's live cultures won't survive reheating; it's here for flavor. Your kimchi, added cold, is the live-culture source.

B2: Shawarma chicken bowls with chickpeas & carrots

5 servings. about 535 kcal, 34 g protein, 12 g fiber (with labneh and 1 pesto cube). 40 minutes

  • 1¼ lb chicken thighs
  • Spice: 2 tsp cumin, 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp coriander, 1 tsp turmeric, ½ tsp cinnamon, ¼ tsp ground cloves, black pepper, salt
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 cans chickpeas, drained and patted dry
  • 1½ lb carrots, cut into sticks
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
  • 2 tbsp pomegranate molasses
  • Garlic labneh: ⅔ cup labneh, 2 garlic cloves, lemon, salt
  • To serve: 1 frozen pesto cube per bowl, ground papaya seed
  1. Oven to 425°F. Mince the labneh garlic and let it rest.
  2. Toss the chicken with half the spice, 1 tbsp oil and the lemon juice. Spread it on a pan with the onion.
  3. Toss carrots and chickpeas with the rest of the spice and oil on a second pan.
  4. Roast both 25–30 minutes, shaking the chickpea pan halfway.
  5. Drizzle pomegranate molasses over the carrots and chickpeas once they're out of the oven.
  6. Stir the garlic into the labneh and portion into 5 cups (2 tbsp each).
  7. Slice the chicken and portion. Labneh and pesto go on after reheating.
B3: Shrimp, broccoli & shiitake stir-fry with soba

4 servings. about 460 kcal, 42 g protein, 8 g fiber. 30 minutes

  • 8 oz 100% buckwheat soba
  • 1¼ lb peeled shrimp, thawed
  • 8 oz fresh broccoli, cut into small florets (peel and slice the stem too)
  • 8 oz shiitake, sliced
  • ½ medium cabbage (about 7 oz), shredded; 3 carrots, cut into matchsticks
  • Summer and fall: 2 sliced bell peppers in place of the cabbage. Spring: a handful of snap peas.
  • 3 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • Sauce: 3 tbsp tamari, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp arrowroot, 2 tbsp water
  • 1 tbsp avocado oil, ½ tbsp toasted sesame oil, lime
  1. Mince the garlic first. Cook the soba one minute less than the package says, rinse it cold, and toss with the sesame oil.
  2. Whisk the sauce.
  3. Heat avocado oil in a wok over high heat. Cook the shiitake 3–4 minutes, then the broccoli and carrots 3 minutes, then the cabbage 2 minutes. Push everything to the side.
  4. Cook the shrimp 2–3 minutes, until just pink. Slightly under is fine, since they cook more when reheated.
  5. Add the garlic and ginger for 30 seconds, then the sauce, and stir about 1 minute until glossy.
  6. Portion over the soba. Reheat at 70% power so the shrimp stay tender.

Baked oat cups (Week B)

Makes 8. 2 cups per serving, for Days 4–7. about 365 kcal, 17 g protein, 6 g fiber. 350°F for 20–22 minutes

  • 2 cups rolled oats
  • 2 eggs
  • ¾ cup Greek yogurt
  • ¾ cup 2% milk
  • 2 tbsp honey (add stevia if you want it sweeter)
  • ¼ cup raw pepitas
  • 2 tbsp ground flax

Mix everything, divide among 8 greased muffin cups, and bake. Freeze them in pairs. Eat cold, or warm them for 30–45 seconds. Baking reduces the pepitas' cucurbitacin, per your guide; your raw pepitas come from the pesto and trail mix.

Home night: sheet-pan salmon (or cod), broccoli & chickpeas

1 serving. about 545 kcal, 44 g protein, 17 g fiber with salmon and broccoli (with cabbage: about 520 kcal, 40 g protein; cod instead of salmon: about 60 kcal less). 25 minutes, mostly hands-off

  • 1 frozen wild salmon portion, about 5 oz (or about 6 oz cod)
  • 10 oz fresh vegetables: broccoli florets early in the week (a crown keeps about 5 days), then cabbage wedges or thick carrot coins, which keep for weeks
  • ½ can chickpeas, drained and patted dry
  • 2 tsp olive oil, divided
  • ½ tsp cumin or smoked paprika, salt, pepper
  • Lemon wedge; your ¼ cup kimchi on the side
  1. Oven to 425°F. Toss the vegetables and chickpeas with 1 tsp oil and the spice on a sheet pan. Roast 10 minutes (cabbage wedges and carrots: 15).
  2. Push them to the edges. Rub the fish with the last teaspoon of oil, salt and pepper, and lay it in the middle.
  3. Roast 10–12 minutes if the fish was thawed, 15–18 if it's straight from the freezer (rinse off any ice first). It's done at 145°F, or when it flakes.
  4. Squeeze the lemon over everything.

Move a portion to the fridge the night before if you think you'll want it. If you forget, cook it from frozen. The dinner container you didn't eat goes back in the freezer as a backup for a triple day or a Sunday you skip prep. The other half-can of chickpeas keeps 3–4 days in the fridge.

Egg muffins (both weeks)

Makes 12. 2 per serving. about 172 kcal, 14 g protein. 350°F for 20–22 minutes

  • 10 eggs and ½ cup 2% cottage cheese, whisked
  • Week A filling: 5 oz spinach, 6 oz cremini, ½ onion
  • Week B filling: 6 oz kale, 2 scallions, ½ onion
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp black seed, salt, pepper

Sauté the filling in oil for 5 minutes, divide it among 12 greased cups, pour in the egg mixture, and top with black seed. Grind papaya seed over them when you eat them. Day 7's snack is a Siggi's instead.

Pepita-cilantro pesto (Week A Sunday)

About 16 tablespoons. 64 kcal per tablespoon

  • ½ cup raw pepitas
  • 2 packed cups cilantro (stems are fine)
  • 3 garlic cloves, rested 10 minutes
  • ⅓ cup olive oil
  • 2 tbsp lime or lemon juice
  • ½ tsp salt, splash of water

Blend until smooth and freeze in tablespoon cubes. That's 5 cubes for the A2 chili, 5 for the B2 bowls, plus a few spares. Garlic in oil must stay frozen or refrigerated, never at room temperature.

Pomegranate vinaigrette (Week A Sunday)

Shake 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses with 3 tbsp olive oil and a pinch of salt. It has no garlic, so it keeps in the fridge for two weeks. Use it on the A3 cauliflower; any extra is good on egg muffins.

Optional extras
  • ¼ avocado (California avocados run spring through early fall) on the chili or a B2 bowl, about 60 kcal and 2.5 g fiber. It's best on home meals, since cut avocado browns in a lunch bag.
  • Pet-sitting pouch: an extra ¼ cup trail mix (185 kcal). On days with all three jobs, also grab a spare Siggi's from a work fridge (110 kcal).

Swaps & seasons

Rainbow buys seasonally, so something on the list will be missing some weeks. The recipes are written around produce that's there all year; these are the stand-ins. The recipes carry the same notes inline, so you don't have to come here mid-cook.

Three rules that keep the numbers honest:

  • Swap by weight, not by piece. A pound of carrots for a pound of peppers keeps calories and fiber within a few grams.
  • Brassicas (cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts) reheat better than peppers, zucchini or summer squash. Save the watery ones for the fridge days, 1 to 3.
  • Leafy greens shrink to almost nothing. If you're swapping a green for a firm vegetable, use about three times the raw weight.

What's out of the base plan on purpose

Tea, broth & stash

Daily drinks

  • Sea moss: one spoonful before you leave. One spoonful is a normal serving; don't add an iodine supplement on top.
  • Protein shake: 1 scoop Ascent chocolate with 1 cup 2% milk. Before you leave, or on gym days mix it in a shaker and drink it at the retail break or after your shift. Keep it cold.
  • Focus tea: first thing at work, or with breakfast on home days. Write your top three tasks while it steeps.
  • Creatine: your usual 3–5 g with water. On gym days, first thing with your sea moss, since it holds you until breakfast. Other days, mid-morning. Timing matters much less than taking it every day.
  • Ginger tea: one cup mid-morning. Keep a jar in each work fridge and refill it weekly.
  • Broth: a home ritual, before dinner. On packed days, skip it rather than rushing the steep.
  • Evening tea: an hour before bed, lid on, screens off, with 1–2 crushed whole cloves. Cloves have no caffeine and don't work against the calming herbs. The flavor is strong, so start with one.
Ginger-cinnamon-clove tea with Nigerian ginger
  • 2 quarts water
  • ¼ cup dried Nigerian ginger slices, cracked in a bag with a rolling pin. Dried ginger is stronger than fresh, so start here and adjust the next batch.
  • 3 cinnamon sticks, 8–10 whole cloves
  • Honey to taste

Simmer covered for 20 minutes. Strain, stir in the honey while it's warm, cool, and jar it. It keeps about 5 days in the fridge, so freeze a jar if you make more.

Broth, per cup (from your prep guide)
  1. Mince 1–2 garlic cloves and let them sit.
  2. Heat 1 cup of broth base to just under a simmer, then turn off the heat.
  3. Add 3–4 bruised sprigs each of oregano (leaves stripped) and thyme. Cover and steep 10–15 minutes.
  4. Strain, then add the garlic in the mug at drinking temperature.

One correction to the guide: carnosic acid from rosemary is fat-soluble, not water-soluble, so the "don't skim the fat" advice helps your rosemary as well as the oregano and thyme.

The one habit that makes this work

Keep two bins on a fridge shelf, one for the gym and one for retail. Each night, check tomorrow's schedule on the Today tab and put the containers in the right bin. In the morning, tip each bin into its insulated bag, add an ice pack, and go. Keep the bags by the door and the ice packs in the freezer door.

If you take any prescription medications, run the tea blends past a pharmacist. Gotu kola and passionflower both have interaction notes, and matcha adds caffeine to anything stimulating.
